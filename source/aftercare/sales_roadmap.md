# thebestbill — Sales Roadmap

> This is your week-by-week playbook for finding, closing, and serving your first 5 paying brands. No fluff. Just what to do each day.

---

## Week 1: Find Brands & Start Conversations

### Day 1-2: Build Your Hit List

Find 50 D2C / mid-market brands that sell physical products with warranties in India.

**Where to find them:**

| Source | How |
|---|---|
| LinkedIn | Search: "founder" + "D2C" + (appliances OR electronics OR kitchen OR home) + India |
| Amazon India | Browse Home & Kitchen, Fans, Water Purifiers. Look at brands with 500-5000 reviews (mid-size, not giants). Note brand names → find founders on LinkedIn. |
| Shark Tank India portfolio | Many are D2C product brands. Google "Shark Tank India season 1/2/3 companies" → filter to physical products. |
| IndiaMART | Search product categories. Find manufacturers who also sell direct. |
| D2C brand lists | Google "top D2C brands India 2024/2025". Filter to physical products. |
| YourStory / Inc42 | Search for D2C brand funding announcements. Recently funded = growing = after-sales pain starting. |

**What to note for each brand:**

```
Brand: Atomberg
Founder: Manoj Meena (LinkedIn)
Products: Ceiling fans, mixer grinders
Sells on: Own website, Amazon, Flipkart
Approx orders/month: ~5,000-10,000 (guess from reviews)
Current support: 1800 number, email, website form
Pain signal: Amazon reviews mentioning support issues? Website complaint form broken?
```

**Goal**: 50 brands in a spreadsheet by end of Day 2.

### Day 3-5: Send 30 DMs

Don't pitch. Ask.

**LinkedIn DM template (cold):**

```
Hi [First Name],

Saw [Brand Name] — love the [specific product/thing you noticed].

Quick question: how does your team handle customer
complaints after purchase right now? Phone, email,
WhatsApp?

I'm researching after-sales for D2C product brands
and trying to understand the real pain points.

Would appreciate a 10-min chat if you're open to it.

— [Your name]
```

**Why this works:**
- Not pitchy. You're asking, not selling.
- "Researching" = non-threatening
- Specific compliment = you actually looked at their brand
- "10-min chat" = low commitment

**LinkedIn DM template (warm — if you have any connection):**

```
Hey [First Name],

We're connected through [mutual/group/event]. I've been
digging into after-sales for product brands in India
and your name came up.

Curious — what does post-purchase support look like
at [Brand]? Phone line, WhatsApp, email?

Would love to hear what's working and what's not.
5-10 min whenever you have time.
```

**Send 30 DMs across Day 3, 4, 5.** (10 per day. Takes ~1 hour/day.)

**Expected response rate**: 15-20% (5-6 replies out of 30).

### Day 5-7: Have the Conversations

When someone replies and agrees to chat, here's your call script:

```
DISCOVERY CALL SCRIPT (10-15 minutes)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

1. OPEN (1 min)
   "Thanks for taking the time. I'll keep this quick.
    I'm building something for D2C brands around
    after-sales and I just want to understand how
    it works for you today."

2. UNDERSTAND CURRENT STATE (5 min)
   Ask these in order. Let them talk.

   • "When a customer has a problem with a [product],
      how do they reach you?"
      [Listen: phone? email? WhatsApp? website form?]

   • "Who handles those complaints on your side?"
      [Listen: founder? support team? outsourced?]

   • "What's the most common type of complaint?"
      [Listen: what breaks, what they ask about]

   • "When a complaint comes in, what info does your
      team need to figure out the issue?"
      [Listen: model number, serial, invoice, purchase date]

   • "How long does it typically take from complaint
      to resolution?"
      [Listen: hours? days? weeks?]

3. FIND THE PAIN (3 min)

   • "What's the most frustrating part of this process?"
      [Listen: this is gold. Write it down verbatim.]

   • "How much time does your team spend just figuring
      out what product the customer has and whether
      it's under warranty?"
      [Listen: if they say "a lot" or "most of the time"
       → you have a wedge]

   • "Do you get any data or patterns from complaints?
      Like which products have the most issues?"
      [Listen: almost always "no"]

4. SOFT PITCH (2 min) — ONLY if they expressed pain

   "Here's what I'm working on. When you sell a product,
    we auto-register it. The customer gets a WhatsApp
    message with their product and warranty info.

    When they have an issue, they message back — and
    we already know who they are, what they bought,
    and whether it's under warranty. We ask 3-4 questions,
    create a clean complaint, and send it to your team
    with full context.

    Your team gets a structured ticket instead of a
    blank 'my fan is broken' message."

5. THE ASK

   "I'd love to try this with your next 50 customers.
    Completely free. I'll handle the WhatsApp setup,
    send the registration messages, and manage complaints
    for 2 weeks. At the end, I'll show you the data.

    If it's useful, we talk about continuing.
    If not, no hard feelings. Worst case you got
    free support for 2 weeks.

    Would you be open to that?"
```

**The answers you're listening for:**

| What they say | What it means |
|---|---|
| "Our support team spends most of the time figuring out what product the customer has" | **STRONG signal.** Your product eliminates this. |
| "We get complaints on WhatsApp but it's chaotic, no structure" | **STRONG signal.** You add structure. |
| "We have no idea which products have the most issues" | **STRONG signal.** Product feedback is your hidden value. |
| "We use Freshdesk/Zendesk and it works fine" | **WEAK signal.** They have a solution. Move on unless it's clearly not working. |
| "After-sales isn't really a priority for us right now" | **NO signal.** Move on. |
| "We barely get complaints" | **NO signal.** Wrong customer. Move on. |

## Week 2: Close First Brand & Start Operating

### Goal: Get 1 brand to give you access to their customer data

**What you need from them:**
1. CSV of last 50-100 orders (name, phone, product, serial, date, price)
2. Their warranty terms per product (how many months)
3. Their service/support email (where you'll send structured complaints)
4. Permission to message their customers via WhatsApp

**What you give them:**
- "I'll register all these customers, send them WhatsApp confirmations, and handle any complaints that come in for the next 2 weeks. Completely free. You just need to resolve the issues I route to you."

### Set Up (Day 1-2 of Week 2)

```
1. WhatsApp Business account set up
   ├── Use brand's name as the business name
   ├── Or "Brand Name Support" if they prefer
   └── Profile photo = brand logo

2. Import customer data
   ├── Paste CSV into Google Sheets
   ├── Columns: name, phone, product, serial, purchase_date,
   │   warranty_months, warranty_end_date
   └── Calculate warranty end dates

3. Send registration messages (manually)
   ├── Go through list one by one
   ├── Send each customer the welcome message
   └── ~50 messages = ~2-3 hours
       "Hi [Name]! 👋 This is [Brand] support.
        Your [Product Name] is registered.
        Warranty valid until [Date] ✅
        Reply here anytime if you need help."
```

### Operate (Day 3-14 of Week 2-3)

```
Customer messages → you handle it:

1. Look up their phone in your sheet
2. Find their product + warranty status
3. Respond with product context
4. Ask diagnostic questions (manually, following the trees)
5. Create complaint summary
6. Email it to brand's service email
7. Track in your sheet: complaint #, status, dates
8. When brand updates → message customer

You are the entire system. This is the point.
```

**What to track (in your sheet):**

| Column | Example |
|---|---|
| Complaint # | TBB-001 |
| Customer | Priya Sharma |
| Phone | +91 98765 43210 |
| Product | Gorilla 1200mm |
| Issue | Grinding noise at speed 3+ |
| Warranty | Active |
| Status | Resolved |
| Created | Jun 8 |
| Resolved | Jun 11 |
| Days to resolve | 3 |
| Customer rating | 5/5 |
| Notes | Motor bearing issue, technician replaced |

## Week 3-4: Show Results & Close the Deal

### The Review Meeting

After 2 weeks of operating, schedule a call with the brand founder.

```
REVIEW CALL SCRIPT
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

"Here's what happened in 2 weeks:

📊 NUMBERS
• 47 customers messaged with registration
• 12 customers had questions
  - 5 warranty status checks (answered instantly by me)
  - 4 product complaints (routed to your team)
  - 2 service requests
  - 1 general question
• Average resolution: 2 days (vs your current ~5 days)
• Customer satisfaction: 4.6/5

💡 PRODUCT INSIGHT
• 3 out of 4 complaints were about Gorilla 1200mm
  motor noise at high speed. Possible batch issue?
  Your team may want to check motors from [supplier]
  in [date range] production.

🕒 TIME SAVED
• Your support team handled 4 pre-qualified complaints
  with full context instead of ~12 raw WhatsApp messages
  that needed 5-8 back-and-forth each to understand.
• Estimated 4-5 hours of support time saved.

This is what I'd like to do going forward:
• Continue handling your WhatsApp support
• Automate the warranty checks and complaint intake
• Give you a dashboard to see everything
• ₹5,000/month or free for 2 more months while
  I build the automation

What do you think?"
```

### Possible Outcomes

| Outcome | What to do |
|---|---|
| "Yes, let's continue and I'll pay" | You have a paying customer. Celebrate. Move to brand #2. |
| "Yes, let's continue but free for now" | Fine. You need the data and case study more than ₹5K. Set a date to revisit pricing. |
| "It's useful but we can't commit right now" | Ask: "What would make this a clear yes?" Listen. Adjust. |
| "Not really useful for us" | Ask: "What was missing? What would have made this valuable?" Learn. Move on. |

## Month 3: Decision Point

At 5 brands, you'll know:

| Question | How you'll know |
|---|---|
| Do brands value this? | Are they willing to pay (or at least begging you not to stop)? |
| What do they value MOST? | Is it the complaint routing? The warranty checks? The product feedback? |
| What don't they care about? | You built something they never use? Cut it. |
| Is this a business? | 5 × ₹5K-15K/month = ₹25K-75K/month. Enough to survive while growing? |
| Can you charge more? | If they'd be lost without you → raise prices. If they'd shrug → lower prices or add value. |

**If 3+ of 5 brands would pay → start building the real product (Layer 2-3).**
**If <3 would pay → figure out why and iterate, or pivot.**

## Common Objections & Responses

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "We already use WhatsApp Business" | "Great — then your customers already message you there. The problem is your team has zero context when they do. They spend 10 minutes figuring out what product, what warranty, what model. I eliminate that. They get a structured ticket with everything." |
| "We use Freshdesk/Zendesk" | "Those are great for ticketing. But they don't know what product the customer owns or whether it's under warranty. I add that context layer. Your Freshdesk tickets go from 'my fan is broken' to a full complaint with product, serial, warranty status, and diagnostic info." |
| "We don't get that many complaints" | "How many per month? Even 20 complaints with full context and product pattern data is more valuable than 20 raw messages. But if support volume is genuinely low, this might not be the right fit for you right now." |
| "Can't share customer data with a third party" | "Totally fair concern. Two options: I can work under NDA, or we can do the pilot with only new customers going forward — no historical data needed." |
| "What's the catch? Why free?" | "I'm building this product and I need real-world data to make it great. Your brand helps me learn. In exchange, you get free support ops for a month. If it works, we'll talk about a paid plan. If not, you got free support." |
| "We'll build this internally" | "You absolutely could. It'll take your eng team 2-3 months — WhatsApp API integration, warranty logic, complaint routing, dashboard. Or you can be live in 2 days with me. What's more valuable right now — your engineers building this, or building your core product?" |
