Steps 1–6 · The customer's side

Months later, something breaks.
One message is all it takes.

Step 1 of 6
01
Registration

Registered before
you leave the store.

The moment you buy a product, it's registered in your name — linked to your phone number, automatically. You get a WhatsApp message with your warranty details, and a small QR sticker goes on your product. That's it. Nothing to fill out later.

🏷️ QR sticker on your product
📱 WhatsApp warranty confirmation
0️⃣ Zero action required from you
02
Lookup

One message.
No triage.

Something goes wrong months later. You message the brand's WhatsApp. The agent already knows who you are from your phone number. It shows you your registered products and asks which one needs attention — you tap to pick. Not a word about model numbers or purchase dates.

🔍 Identified by phone number
📦 All your products shown
⏱️ 0 minutes of triage
03
Manual

Real help from
the actual manual.

Every answer comes from that product's actual user manual — not a generic FAQ, not a canned response. One real, doable step with a reference to the exact section it came from. If it doesn't fix it, the next step comes from the manual too.

📖 Grounded in your product's manual
🎯 One specific, actionable step
🔗 Source section shown
04
Back & forth

It follows up.
It doesn't forget.

It waits for your reply. Fixed? Great — no ticket, no wait. Still broken? The next real step. The agent follows the actual conversation, not a rigid script. It adapts to what you report back, step by step, until the issue is resolved.

🔄 Adapts to your responses
💬 Full conversation memory
✅ Resolved with no ticket if possible
05
Human handoff

Not fixed?
A person takes over.

Safety isn't the only reason. If two manual steps don't fix it, if your problem isn't in the manual at all, or if the same problem is back after a fix — the agent stops and hands over. It never guesses. The ticket arrives with what was tried, and why.

🔁 Two steps tried, still broken
📖 Not in the manual — no guessing
↩️ Same problem back after a fix
06
Safety

When it's serious,
people take over.

A burning smell. Sparking. An exposed wire. These skip the AI entirely — a pre-filled ticket goes to a human immediately. Your name, your product, your exact words are already captured. You don't have to explain again. A technician is dispatched.

⚠️ Safety flag triggers instantly
📋 Pre-filled ticket, no re-explanation
👤 Human technician dispatched

Under the hood

The model words it.
Code decides the rest.

A small local model can phrase a manual step well and judge a safety call badly. So every decision that matters is a rule, a query or a policy. Each card below is checked live, right now.

1

API Gateway + Lambda

This whole site, the pages and every API route, is one Lambda behind API Gateway in SAM Local. Logs and CloudWatch metrics via Lambda Powertools.

checking…
2

Strands Agents

Phrases one manual step and reads the customer's reply, on a local Ollama model. A fresh agent per call, timed by hooks.

checking…
3

OpenSearch

Finds the right manual section (BM25), spots a recurring problem with a time-window query, and counts why customers escalate.

checking…
4

Cedar

Decides what each signed-in person may see and change. Every answer names the policy that decided it.

checking…
5

DynamoDB

One table holds the customer registry, conversations, tickets, the WhatsApp message log and cases, with an atomic ticket counter. Here it is DynamoDB Local.

checking…

Never left to the model

  • Safety is a keyword rule, or the manual's own safety section, that skips troubleshooting.
  • Which step comes next is read from the manual's numbered list.
  • Two tries, then a person is a counter.
  • Not in the manual is a word-overlap test.
  • Seen it before is an OpenSearch query.
  • Warranty dates are arithmetic.
  • Who sees what is a Cedar policy.

Real

The order-file check and customer registry, the agent, manual search, escalation rules, tickets, the chat log and inbox where a person replies, Cedar policies, DynamoDB and the Lambda, all running on this machine.

Simulated

WhatsApp's network (the phone talks to a Meta-shaped webhook on this server), the QR sticker, and the customers, orders and manuals, which are all fabricated.

Not used

An AWS account. Nothing here is deployed. Bedrock, Verified Permissions and OpenSearch Serverless are where each piece would go.
