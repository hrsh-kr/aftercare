# Aftercare

### The support line that already knows what you bought.

---

## The problem

Something in your house breaks. A fan starts grinding. A geyser stops heating. You dig out a WhatsApp number from a box you kept, or you don't, and you message the brand.

"Hi, my fan is making a noise."

"Sure, can you share the model number?"

You don't have it. You get up, find the fan, read tiny text off a sticker on the underside, type it in.

"When did you purchase this?"

You don't remember exactly. You dig through email for the order confirmation. You find it, copy the date.

"Do you have proof of purchase?"

You screenshot the invoice. You send it. Fifteen minutes have passed and you haven't even described what's actually wrong yet — you've just proven, from scratch, that you own the thing you're asking about. Every single time you contact them, for the life of the product, you do this again. The brand has all of this information already. They sold you the fan. They know when. They have no way to connect that sale to the stranger messaging them at 9pm with a grinding noise.

This isn't a rare, unlucky interaction. It's the default shape of after-sales support for nearly every appliance, gadget, and physical product sold in India — the customer becomes the one responsible for proving their own history to a company that already has it on file. The support agent on the other end isn't being difficult. They're starting from zero on every single conversation, because nothing connects the person typing to the product they bought.

Multiply this by every appliance in every home, every year something breaks. The brand loses time triaging basic facts instead of solving the actual problem. The customer loses patience before the real conversation even starts. Neither side wanted this — it's just what happens when a sale and a support conversation live in two systems that have never once talked to each other.

## What we're building

**Aftercare connects the two.** A product gets registered once, quietly, at the point of sale — no app, no form the customer has to fill out later. When that customer messages support, months or years afterward, the conversation already knows exactly what they bought, when, and its warranty status. No detective work. No screenshots.

But knowing the product isn't the interesting part — any spreadsheet can do that. The part that actually changes the conversation: when the customer describes what's wrong, in their own words, however messy — "it's making a weird grinding sound when I turn it up" — the system reads that against the *actual manual for that exact product* and tries a real, grounded fix first, the way a genuinely knowledgeable support person would, before ever creating a ticket for a human. Not a script. Not a menu of five canned options. A real answer, grounded in the real manual, for the real product this specific person owns.

If it can't resolve it — or if what's described sounds urgent or unsafe — it doesn't guess. It escalates immediately, with everything already gathered: the product, the history, the specific issue, and exactly what's already been tried.

## Why both sides want this

**The customer** gets treated like someone the brand actually recognizes, not a stranger who has to reprove themselves every time. A real answer, fast, in the same conversation they were already having.

**The brand's support team** stops doing the same fifteen minutes of triage on every single conversation, and starts every ticket that does reach them already knowing what's wrong, what's been tried, and how urgent it is.

## Where this goes next

Every resolved conversation becomes a fact the brand didn't have before: which issue keeps coming up on which product, how often it's the same fault, whether it's safety-relevant. That's the thing a generic support inbox can never produce, because it never connects one conversation to the next the way Aftercare does from the very first message.
